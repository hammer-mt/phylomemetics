## Bottom line

- **The field the paper wants to join decided long ago that it does not need a discrete unit.** Henrich & Boyd (2002) show formally that continuous, error-prone, reconstructed representations still give replicator-like dynamics when attractors are strong. So "memetics stalled for lack of a unit" is true of memetics and false of cultural evolution. The defensible claim for P1 is narrower: neurons are an **automatic trait assay** (a way to measure z), not a replicator. Any z works in the Price equation, including TF-IDF, so the neuron assay has to be *better*, not merely *possible*.
- **The best validation targets are known-answer tests with ground truth that does not come from the method**: randomised headline experiments (Upworthy archive, 32,487 experiments), human and LLM transmission chains (content biases), and documented descent (citations, remakes). These fix E3's power problem (n = 15 early works, power 0.27 at rho 0.3; `study/results/experiments.json` → `E3_power_simulated`).
- **Transmission chains are the natural home for the neuron claim**, because each step is a paraphrase: wording changes, ideas persist. That is exactly the regime the paper says it needs (`study/paper/paper.md` §4.2). If neurons cannot beat word overlap and an LLM reader on chain retention, P1 has little left as a *measurement* claim.
- **Cultural attraction theory reads the study's E1 null as a positive finding against lineage-specific inheritance.** The name-free Batman core is the superhero template and does not separate Batman from other hero films (AUC 0.52–0.54, `study/README.md`, from `study/results/experiments.json` → `qwen3-8b.E1.A_name_free.hero_film_cousins.auc` = 0.515 and `llama3.1-8b.E1.A_name_free.hero_film_cousins.auc` = 0.544). Convergence on an attractor produces similarity without descent. Any phylogeny built from such units is exposed to homoplasy.
- **Several famous results are contested and should not be used as "accepted science"**: population size and cumulative culture (Vaesen et al. 2016; Fay et al. 2019 against Henrich 2004; Kline & Boyd 2010), neutral-model inference from frequency distributions (Leroi et al. 2020; Kandler et al. 2017; Karjus et al. 2020), conformity from population-level data (Acerbi et al. 2016), MCI (Purzycki & Willard 2015), and the sign of valence effects (Berger & Milkman 2012 positive more shared; Robertson et al. 2023 negative more clicked).
- **I found no published study that uses LLM-internal neurons or features as the trait measure in a cultural-evolution analysis** (arXiv queries on "cultural evolution"/"cultural attraction" with interpretability/SAE returned nothing on point). The nearest are Movva et al. 2025 (SAE features on text embeddings for hypothesis generation, already ref [25] of the paper) and LLM transmission-chain work that measures outputs only. That is an open niche, with a limited search behind the claim. `[checker: the absence claim could not be re-tested, WebSearch exhausted; UNVERIFIED as a negative. Confirmed from the Movva et al. PDF (arXiv:2502.04382, ICML 2025) that their HEADLINES dataset is the Upworthy Research Archive, so feature-based analysis of target 1's data is already published, with SAE features on text embeddings, not LLM neurons.]`

## Findings

### A. The theoretical core: what is and is not required

1. **Cavalli-Sforza & Feldman (1981)**, *Cultural Transmission and Evolution* (doi:10.1515/9780691209357). Transmission modes (vertical, oblique, horizontal) give different rates of change and between-group variance; vertical is slow and conservative, horizontal is fast and epidemic-like. Empirical companion: Cavalli-Sforza, Feldman, Chen & Dornbusch 1982, *Science* (doi:10.1126/science.7123211), parent–offspring trait correlations from a Stanford survey `[detail from memory, UNVERIFIED]`. `[checker: Crossref abstract confirms a survey of transmission components across "a variety of traits" which found "religion and politics are mostly determined in the family", a mode giving high stability and high variation between and within groups. That the respondents were Stanford students, and that the statistic was a parent–offspring correlation, are not in the abstract: still UNVERIFIED.]` *Neuron test:* estimate a parent–offspring regression per unit on documented source→adaptation pairs. The slope is a transmission coefficient. *Data:* any corpus with documented descent. *Rival:* same regression on word presence.
2. **Boyd & Richerson (1985)**, *Culture and the Evolutionary Process* (book; existence confirmed via review doi:10.2307/2803086); Richerson & Boyd 2005 (*Not by Genes Alone*; doi:10.7208/chicago/9780226712130.001.0001; `[checker: Crossref dates this record 2004; the book carries a 2005 imprint]`). Three bias families. Content (direct) bias: variant adopted for what it is. Model-based (prestige, success): Henrich & Gil-White 2001 (doi:10.1016/s1090-5138(00)00071-4). Frequency-dependent (conformist): Henrich & Boyd 1998 (doi:10.1016/s1090-5138(98)00018-x); prediction is a sigmoidal adoption curve, disproportionate copying of the majority. Blunt status: conformity **cannot be identified from population-level signatures** (Acerbi, van Leeuwen, Haun & Tennie 2016, doi:10.1038/srep36068); lab evidence is mixed (Efferson et al. 2008, doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2007.08.003; Muthukrishna, Morgan & Henrich 2016, doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2015.05.004) `[checker: both papers exist with these titles; no abstract was available, so "mixed" is the note-writer's characterisation, UNVERIFIED]`. Prestige evidence outside the lab is thinner than the theory's fame (review: Jiménez & Mesoudi 2019, doi:10.1057/s41599-019-0228-7) `[checker: the abstract says only that the review examines "the evidence for and against the use of prestige bias" and points out "limitations in the current literature"; "thinner outside the lab" is an interpretation, UNVERIFIED]`. E3 in the study was in effect a success-bias test and found nothing (rho 0.25 Qwen, 0.00 Llama; original texts −0.32 and −0.50; `study/results/experiments.json` → `qwen3-8b.E3.primary.rho` = 0.254, `llama3.1-8b.E3.primary.rho` = 0.004, `qwen3-8b.E3.original_text.rho` = −0.321, `llama3.1-8b.E3.original_text.rho` = −0.500; `[checker: path corrected, E3 sits under each model key, there is no top-level E3]`).
3. **Henrich & Boyd (2002)**, "On modeling cognition and culture: Why cultural evolution does not require replication of representations", *J. Cogn. Cult.* 2(2) (doi:10.1163/156853702320281836). Abstract confirmed (and re-confirmed by the checker; the abstract's second model is stated as "very low fidelity replication of representations at the individual level does not preclude accurate replication at the population level"): grants Sperber's premises (non-discrete, inaccurate, reconstructed) and shows with three models that (i) with strong attractors relative to selective forces a continuous model **reduces to the discrete-trait replicator model**, (ii) selective learning can still give stable traditions, (iii) and cumulative adaptation. *Quantitative prediction for P1:* if neuron units are natural units, their values across a population of texts should cluster (multimodal), and inheritance from two sources should look like segregation, not blending. If values are unimodal and children sit at the parental mean, the right model is quantitative genetics on continuous traits, and "meme = neuron fires" is a threshold artefact. The study binarises with a presence rule (`study/discover.py`), and its family count moves with model and threshold (14,843 / 24,732 / 5,641; `study/README.md`), which is what a thresholded continuous trait would do.
4. **Price-equation approaches.** El Mouden, André, Morin & Nettle 2014 (doi:10.1111/jeb.12296); Nettle 2020 (doi:10.1098/rstb.2019.0358); worked empirical use in MacCallum, Mauch, Burt & Leroi 2012, DarwinTunes (doi:10.1073/pnas.1203182109). Δz̄ = Cov(w, z)/w̄ + E(wΔz)/w̄: selection plus transmission. Needs only a trait measure, an ancestor–descendant map and a fitness count. Nettle (abstract confirmed): design-like properties in culture "may owe more to transmission biases than to cultural selection". *Discriminating prediction:* replicator memetics expects the transmission term ≈ 0; attraction theory expects |transmission| ≥ |selection| for most traits. *Neuron test:* compute both terms per unit and per word on the same lineage; compare the share of change carried by each term, and unit "heritability" under paraphrase.
5. **Mesoudi.** Mesoudi, Whiten & Laland 2006 (doi:10.1017/s0140525x06009083; already ref [4]); *Cultural Evolution* 2011 (doi:10.7208/chicago/9780226520452.001.0001); Mesoudi 2021 "Cultural selection and biased transformation: two dynamics" (doi:10.1098/rstb.2020.0053), the current statement that both operate and must be separated empirically. Acerbi & Mesoudi 2015 (doi:10.1007/s10539-015-9490-2) on what the California/Paris disagreement really is.

### B. Cultural attraction theory (Paris school)

6. **Sperber 1996** *Explaining Culture* (existence via review doi:10.1353/lan.1998.0196); Claidière & Sperber 2007 (doi:10.1163/156853707x171829); Claidière, Scott-Phillips & Sperber 2014 (doi:10.1098/rstb.2013.0368); Scott-Phillips, Blancke & Heintz 2018 (doi:10.1002/evan.21716); Morin 2016 *How Traditions Live and Die* (existence via review doi:10.1558/sols.33904); Buskell 2017 (doi:10.1007/s10539-017-9570-6).
7. **Formal signature.** Acerbi, Charbonneau, Miton & Scott-Phillips 2021, "Culture without copying or selection" (doi:10.1017/ehs.2021.47). Abstract confirmed: stability can arise from convergent transformation alone, and non-random transformation and high-fidelity copying "can have different evolutionary signatures at population level" `[checker: quote corrected, the abstract says "can have"]`. *Prediction:* independent lineages started from different seeds converge (between-lineage variance falls with generation); under copying plus selection seed differences persist and change comes from differential survival.
8. **Empirical cases.** Morin 2013, direct gaze in portraits (doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2013.01.004); Miton, Claidière & Mercier 2015, bloodletting (doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2015.01.003); Miton et al. 2020, rhythm (doi:10.1098/rspb.2020.2001); Morin 2018, cardinal orientations in scripts (doi:10.1111/cogs.12550); Sobchuk & Tinits 2020, film anachronies (doi:10.1163/15685373-12340082). Text case with **public data**: Lerique & Roth 2018 on MemeTracker quotations (doi:10.1111/cogs.12494; data from Leskovec, Backstrom & Kleinberg 2009, doi:10.1145/1557019.1557077; `[checker: the abstract does not name the dataset, but Crossref's reference list for Lerique & Roth includes this DOI]`). Abstract confirmed: word substitutions when quotes are copied move toward easier-to-recall words, consistent with attractors. Note the attractor there is **lexical**, so word-level rivals are strong.
9. **What it means for the Batman results.** E1: the name-free core equals the superhero template and fails against hero films, while a TF-IDF centroid separates them at AUC 0.80 (`study/results/experiments.json` → `lexical_comparators.E1_tfidf_centroid.A_name_free.hero_film_cousins.auc` = 0.801). An attraction theorist would say the neurons found the attractor (genre template) and words found the lineage-specific residue (crime-city vocabulary). Also: every text passed through one extra transmission step, a 2020s Wikipedia editor. That step is itself an attractor (house style; the paper admits some neurons track it, §4.4), and it inflates similarity among all works.

### C. Neutral random-copying model

10. **Core papers.** Neiman 1995 (doi:10.2307/282074); Hahn & Bentley 2003, baby names (doi:10.1098/rsbl.2003.0045); Bentley, Hahn & Shennan 2004 (doi:10.1098/rspb.2004.2746); Bentley, Lipo, Herzog & Hahn 2007 (doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2006.10.002). *Predictions:* power-law variant frequencies under random copying with innovation rate μ; turnover z of a top-y list roughly z ≈ y·√μ and independent of N `[formula from memory, UNVERIFIED]` `[checker: confirmed at second hand. Evans & Giometto's full text (https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.4044) reports Bentley et al. 2007 as finding z = √μ·y, independent of N, by numerical analysis of the Wright–Fisher model, with support in baby names and dog breeds; in their own notation, counting entries and exits, it is z = 2·√μ·y. Bentley et al.'s own text was not read.]`. Evans & Giometto 2011 (arXiv:1105.4044; abstract confirmed) show Bentley's formula is only an approximation under Wright–Fisher and **not valid under Moran**; their corrected fit is z ∝ μ^0.55·y^0.86·N^0.13 `[exponents from memory, UNVERIFIED]` `[checker: confirmed from the PDF, eq. 7: z = d·μ^a·y^b·N^c with a = 0.550(2), b = 0.860(1), c = 0.130(2), d = 1.38(2), Wright–Fisher only; they state Bentley's simple form "is excluded statistically"]`. Acerbi & Bentley 2014 (doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2014.02.003): the shape of the turnover profile z(y) separates neutral from conformist and anti-conformist copying `[detail from memory, UNVERIFIED]` `[checker: confirmed from the repository copy (https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/20332/2/FullText.pdf): profile z = a·y^b with b = 0.86 under neutrality; "content bias and conformist bias produce 'convex' turnover profiles", anti-conformist bias a "concave" one. Note that content bias and conformity give the same shape, so the profile does not separate those two. Data: colour terms in 20th-century English books, US baby names, Last.fm.]`. *Public data:* US SSA baby names; Billboard charts; Google Books n-grams (Ruck et al. 2017, arXiv:1703.10698, doi:10.1142/s0219525917500126: a simple neutral model fails, a two-stage "canon within a population" neutral model fits).
11. **Why this is a weak validation target.** Equifinality: Kandler, Wilder & Fortunato 2017 (doi:10.1098/rsos.170949); Crema, Kandler & Shennan 2016 (doi:10.1038/srep39122); Leroi et al. 2020 "Neutral syndrome" (doi:10.1038/s41562-020-0844-7) `[gist from memory: frequency-distribution tests of neutrality have little power, UNVERIFIED]` `[checker: confirmed from the abstract (Europe PMC record for this DOI): "We investigate the most widely used tests of neutrality, show that they are weak and suggest more powerful methods… the ability of neutral models to fit low-information distributions should not be taken as evidence for the absence of selection."]`. O'Dwyer & Kandler 2017 (doi:10.1098/rstb.2016.0426; preprint arXiv:1702.08506; abstract confirmed): progeny distributions have a power-law phase (exponent −3/2 `[value from memory]` `[checker: confirmed, the arXiv abstract reads "a constant exponent of -3/2"]`) then an exponential cut-off, and **analysing only the most popular variants misleads**. Time-series tests: Newberry, Ahern, Clark & Plotkin 2017, Frequency Increment Test (doi:10.1038/nature24455); Karjus et al. 2020 (doi:10.5334/gjgl.909; abstract confirmed): FIT results on single series are sensitive to temporal binning. Generative inference is the accepted route: Kandler & Powell 2018 (doi:10.1098/rstb.2017.0056); Mesoudi & Lycett 2009 (doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2008.07.005).
12. **Neuron-specific traps.** The neutral model is unit-agnostic, so it validates nothing about P1; it is the *null* for a selection test. Three traps: (a) the unit count depends on the merge threshold, and μ is estimated from the count of new units, so the verdict can flip with the threshold; (b) units are correlated (families merged by co-firing; memeplex bundles), which violates the independent-variant assumption exactly as linked selection does in genetics; (c) 32 works is orders of magnitude too few. The paper's planned "mutation and selection against a neutral model" (§4.5) needs thousands of dated works and an approximate-Bayesian-computation design.

### D. Transmission chains and content biases

13. **Method.** Bartlett 1932 *Remembering* (doi:10.1017/cbo9780511759185); Mesoudi & Whiten 2008 review (doi:10.1098/rstb.2008.0129). Bayesian theory: Griffiths & Kalish 2007 (doi:10.1080/15326900701326576), chains of Bayesian learners converge to the prior; Xu & Griffiths 2010 (doi:10.1016/j.cogpsych.2009.09.002); Kirby, Cornish & Smith 2008 (doi:10.1073/pnas.0707835105). This is the formal bridge between attraction and iterated learning: the attractor is the learner's prior.
14. **Results (abstracts confirmed unless tagged).** Social bias: Mesoudi, Whiten & Dunbar 2006 (doi:10.1348/000712605x85871): social information transmitted with greater accuracy and quantity than non-social; gossip no better than everyday social interaction. Stereotype consistency: Kashima 2000 (doi:10.1177/0146167200267007): in five-person chains, stereotype-consistent information is retained better toward the end of the chain even where early positions favour inconsistent information; Lyons & Kashima 2003 (doi:10.1037/0022-3514.85.6.989). Urban legends: Stubbersfield, Tehrani & Flynn 2015 (doi:10.1111/bjop.12073): social, survival and combined legends recalled better than control (60 participants), **but no difference in the choose-to-receive or choose-to-transmit phases** (30 participants). Emotional selection: Heath, Bell & Sternberg 2001 (doi:10.1037/0022-3514.81.6.1028): disgust raises willingness to pass legends on and predicts their spread on websites `[detail from memory, UNVERIFIED]` `[checker: confirmed from the abstract (OpenAlex record for this DOI): Studies 1 and 2, people more willing to pass along more disgusting versions; Study 3, legends with more disgust motifs "were distributed more widely on urban legend Web sites"]`; Eriksson & Coultas 2014 (doi:10.1163/15685373-12342107): disgust operates in all three phases. Negativity: Bebbington et al. 2017 (doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2016.07.004); Fessler, Pisor & Navarrete 2014 (doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0095167); Blaine & Boyer 2018 (doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2017.10.001). Prestige with content: Berl et al. 2021 (doi:10.1017/ehs.2021.37). Review: Stubbersfield 2022 (doi:10.1556/2055.2022.00024). Blunt: the biases are qualitatively robust in recall; effect sizes are modest, and which phase carries the bias differs between studies.
15. **Minimally counterintuitive concepts.** Boyer & Ramble 2001 (doi:10.1207/s15516709cog2504_2); Barrett & Nyhof 2001 (doi:10.1163/156853701300063589); Norenzayan, Atran, Faulkner & Schaller 2006 (doi:10.1207/s15516709cog0000_68). Abstract confirmed: an MCI template (mostly intuitive concepts, a minority counterintuitive) gives a recall advantage after one week, and culturally successful Grimm tales fit the template more than unsuccessful ones. *Prediction:* inverted U in the number of counterintuitive elements. *Public data:* Grimm tales are public domain. Contested: Purzycki & Willard 2015 (doi:10.1080/2153599x.2015.1024915; `[checker: online 2015, Crossref dates the issue 2016, *Religion, Brain & Behavior*; abstract: "serious problems facing the approach"]`); context effects, Upal et al. 2007 (doi:10.1080/15326900701326568).
16. **Online sharing.** Berger & Milkman 2012 (doi:10.1509/jmr.10.0353). Abstract confirmed: three months of New York Times articles; positive content more viral than negative; high-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety) raise virality, sadness lowers it. **Robertson et al. 2023** (doi:10.1038/s41562-023-01538-4). Abstract confirmed: randomised trials (N = 22,743), ~105,000 headline variations, ~5.7 million clicks, >370 million impressions; each additional negative word raises click-through by 2.3% for an average-length headline; positive words lower it. Data: **Upworthy Research Archive**, Matias et al. 2021 (doi:10.1038/s41597-021-00934-7): 32,487 experiments, 150,817 arms, 538,272,878 assignments, open access. Also Youngblood et al. 2023, negativity in voter-fraud tweets (doi:10.1057/s41599-023-02106-x); Vosoughi, Roy & Aral 2018 (doi:10.1126/science.aap9559); Tan, Lee & Pang 2014, topic- and author-controlled tweet pairs (doi:10.3115/v1/p14-1017).

20. **Two consequences.** (a) Circularity: reading a model's chain with that model's own neurons measures the model's prior twice. Use model A's chains, model B's neurons, and human chains. (b) A behavioural handle on P2: if models converge on the same features, their chains should converge on the *same* attractors. Perez et al. report model- and size-dependence, which is mild evidence against strong P2 at the level of behaviour.

The published version, and the one the paper's ref [20] cites, is *Phys. Rev. X* 4, 041036, doi:10.1103/PhysRevX.4.041036; abstract: close to 50 million publication records from Web of Science, PubMed Central and APS]`): n-grams scored by frequency times propagation along citations. 25. **Cautionary tale on instruments.** Hamilton, Leskovec & Jurafsky 2016 claimed statistical laws of semantic change from diachronic embeddings (doi:10.18653/v1/p16-1141); Dubossarsky, Weinshall & Grossman 2017, "Outta control" (doi:10.18653/v1/d17-1118), argued the laws were partly artefacts of the embedding method. A "law of memes" found with one model's neurons needs the same control: shuffled-time corpora and a second instrument.

## What this means for P1 and P2

**P1 (the unit).**
- The accepted science does not ask for a unit, so finding one earns no credit by itself. The question the field will ask is the Price-equation one: is the neuron-based z more heritable, more predictive of fitness, or more interpretable than cheaper z's? On the only lineage tested, it was not more heritable than words: share of comics beating the source 16% (Qwen) and 10% (Llama) against TF-IDF 11.0% and word presence 16.7% (`study/results/experiments.json` → `lexical_comparators.E2_tfidf_cosine.primary.mean_share_beating_source`, `E2_word_presence.primary.mean_share_beating_source`; neuron figures from `study/README.md`; `[checker: all four confirmed. The neuron figures are also in the same file: `qwen3-8b.E2.primary.mean_share_beating_source` = 0.164, `llama3.1-8b.E2.primary.mean_share_beating_source` = 0.102; TF-IDF 0.110, word presence 0.167]`).
- Henrich & Boyd give P1 a testable form: discreteness is an empirical property (strong attractors), not a definition. Nobody has checked whether the study's units are multimodal or thresholded continua. Until then "meme = neuron fires" is a modelling convenience.
- Attraction theory predicts the failure mode already seen: the units that are most widely shared are genre attractors, which carry little lineage signal. P1 holds best for rare, lineage-specific content; that is also where name and word detectors dominate (24 of the 33 strongest twins are name or place detectors by hand reading, `study/README.md`).
- Rewording is the transmission-chain operation. 11 of 31 units survive it in Qwen where 5 or 6 would by chance (`study/README.md`; `study/results/wording.json`, `study/results/robustness/nulls.json`; `[checker: confirmed, `f3_wording.qwen3-8b.percentile.survivors` = 11, `mismatched_null_mean` = 5.006, p = 0.002; Llama has 9 survivors]`). That is a transmission fidelity of roughly one third for hand-picked units, low for something proposed as a unit of inheritance.

**P2 (generality).**
- Cultural evolution offers a behavioural test of P2 that needs no neuron mapping: do different models' transmission chains converge on the same attractors? Perez et al. say attractors depend on model and size. Zhu & Griffiths say GPT-4's elicited priors qualitatively match human priors. Both are compatible with partial convergence on coarse properties and divergence on detail.
- The study's cross-model twins fall from median r = 0.82 to 0.14 once names are removed (`study/README.md`, from `study/results/robustness/nulls.json` → `f2_agreement.sets.top33.r_orig.memes` = 0.824 and `f2_agreement.sets.top33.r_anon_fixed_twin.memes` = 0.141; `[checker: confirmed; this is the 33 strongest Qwen memes with the twin pairing held fixed. A fresh twin search on name-free text gives 0.537 (`r_anon_fresh_search.memes`), so "0.14" is the fixed-pair figure]`). In this field's terms, what is shared across models is the lexical layer, which is the layer that needs no model.
- The semantic-change literature (Hamilton 2016 against Dubossarsky 2017) shows that "laws" can be artefacts of one instrument. P2 has to be shown per finding, by repeating the analysis with a second model's units, not assumed from representation-similarity results.

**Conditions under which P1 and P2 are most likely to hold:** content with strong cognitive attractors (emotion, threat, social relations, ontological violation), measured under paraphrase, with fitness from randomised or documented outcomes. Those are also the conditions where an LLM reader is strongest, so the comparison is unavoidable.

## Experiment seeds

1. **Known-answer selection on randomised headlines.** *Question:* does a neuron pipeline recover the published negativity effect and predict winners beyond words? *Data:* Upworthy Research Archive (32,487 experiments; open access; doi:10.1038/s41597-021-00934-7). *Method:* choose units on the exploratory portion; on held-out experiments score each arm, predict the higher click-through arm; report accuracy and the click-through change per unit of the top negativity unit. *Rival to beat:* negative-word lexicon count, TF-IDF logistic model, sentence-embedding classifier, an LLM asked to pick the winner, headline length. *Falsifier:* neuron accuracy within 1 percentage point of the best rival on at least 3,000 held-out pairs, or no unit among the top 20 that a blind judge labels as negativity. *Cost:* M (one extraction pass over ~150,000 short texts). *Note:* arms differ in wording by design, so words are the treatment; this is a calibration test, not a "beyond words" test. `[checker: add a rival. Movva et al. 2025 (arXiv:2502.04382, ICML 2025; code https://github.com/rmovva/HypotheSAEs) already ran SAE features of text embeddings on this archive (their HEADLINES dataset cites Matias et al. 2021), so the neuron pipeline must beat HypotheSAEs too. The archive is described by its authors as "organized to support exploratory and confirmatory research", which fits the split this seed assumes; the exact split sizes were not checked.]`
2. **Unit survival in transmission chains.** *Question:* do neuron units track what survives retelling when the wording changes at every step? *Data:* Acerbi & Stubbersfield (2023) chain materials and outputs (OSF; exact repository URL not checked); human chain transcripts from the original studies where shared. *Method:* extract units on generation 0; for each later generation compute per-unit survival; test (a) whether blind-labelled class (negative, social, threat, stereotype-consistent, counterintuitive, neutral) predicts survival, (b) whether unit presence predicts human-coded proposition retention. Read chains with a model that did not generate them. *Rival to beat:* content-word overlap with generation 0, sentence-embedding similarity, an LLM coding each proposition as retained or lost. *Falsifier:* unit-based retention prediction no better than word overlap (difference in AUC under 0.03), or class does not predict survival in the direction the five published biases give in at least 4 of 5. *Cost:* S to M.
3. **Do chains converge, and on the same place across models?** *Question:* is change in unit space directional (attraction) and is the attractor shared across models (P2)? *Data:* regenerate Perez et al. style chains from public seeds with two or three open models (generation only; no persuasion optimisation), plus MemeTracker quotation families. *Method:* 50 seeds × 20 chains × 10 generations per model; between-chain variance per unit by generation; cosine between models' mean drift vectors after mapping units to blind labels. *Rival to beat:* the same statistics on TF-IDF vectors and on mean-pooled sentence embeddings. *Falsifier:* variance does not fall (slope ≥ 0) for more than half of units, or cross-model drift agreement is no higher than between two shuffled-label controls. *Cost:* M.
4. **Price decomposition: fidelity against transformation.** *Question:* is change in unit frequency along documented descent carried by selection or by transmission, and are neuron units transmitted more faithfully than words? *Data:* a citation graph with abstracts (open metadata) or a public remake-pair list; at least 5,000 parent–child pairs. *Method:* per unit, Cov(w, z) and E(wΔz) with w = number of descendants; parent–offspring regression slope as fidelity; bootstrap by lineage. *Rival to beat:* the identical decomposition on word presence and on embedding dimensions. *Falsifier:* median fidelity of neuron units not above that of frequency-matched words, or transmission term larger than selection term for more than 80% of units (then "replicator" language should be dropped). *Cost:* M.
5. **Are the units discrete?** *Question:* do unit values cluster and segregate, as Henrich & Boyd require for the replicator approximation? *Data:* the study's existing activations (no new extraction if they are still on disk; `study/.gitignore` excludes them from the repository) and works with two documented sources. *Method:* Hartigan dip test on per-sentence unit maxima; for two-source works, regress child on mean of parents against max of parents. *Rival to beat:* SAE or transcoder features on the same texts; principal components of sentence embeddings as the continuous baseline. *Falsifier:* fewer than 20% of units reject unimodality after correction, or the mean-of-parents model fits better for most units. *Cost:* S.
6. **Scientific memes, head to head with the published n-gram method.** *Question:* do neuron units find inherited ideas that the Kuhn–Perc–Helbing n-gram score misses? *Data:* open abstracts with open citation links, one field, at least 200,000 papers. *Method:* compute the propagation score for n-grams and for neuron units; test on held-out years; focus on ideas whose term changed. *Rival to beat:* the n-gram meme score; embedding nearest-neighbour inheritance; an LLM tagging concepts on a sample. *Falsifier:* neuron units' propagation scores no higher than frequency-matched n-grams, and no gain on term-change cases. *Cost:* L (extraction over a large corpus) or M on a 20,000-paper subsample.
7. **A neutral null that survives the threshold.** *Question:* which units spread more than random copying predicts? *Data:* tens of thousands of dated film plot summaries; trope tags from PicTropes (arXiv:1809.10959) as an independent unit set. *Method:* agent-based neutral and biased copying models fitted by approximate Bayesian computation to progeny distributions and turnover profiles, repeated across merge thresholds and with correlated units collapsed to bundles. *Rival to beat:* the same inference on keyword and trope-tag frequencies. *Falsifier:* the neutral-or-biased verdict flips across reasonable thresholds, or neuron units flag the same winners as keywords. *Cost:* M to L.
8. **Minimal counterintuitiveness in Grimm tales.** *Question:* can units count ontological violations and reproduce the inverted U? *Data:* Grimm tales (public domain) with the success measure of Norenzayan et al. (2006). *Method:* select violation units on other folktales; count per tale; quadratic fit of success on count. *Rival to beat:* an LLM counting violations; a keyword list of magical terms; tale length. *Falsifier:* unit counts correlate with hand counts below r = 0.5, or the quadratic term is not negative. *Cost:* S. Small n; contested theory; cheap enough to run anyway.
9. **Typicality and success at release.** *Question:* do works just past the familiar do best (paper §4.3)? *Data:* film plot summaries with release-time box office; at least 10,000 films. *Method:* atypicality as distance from the genre-year centroid in unit space; regress log revenue on atypicality and its square with budget, year and genre controls. *Rival to beat:* the same distance in LDA-topic space (the published method), TF-IDF and sentence-embedding space. *Falsifier:* unit-space atypicality adds less than 0.5% explained variance over the best rival, or the sign disagrees with the rivals. *Cost:* M.
10. **Recency against Lindy.** *Question:* is the age of borrowed material better described by the known recency preference or by Mike's Lindy and 30-year-return hypotheses? *Data:* dated adaptation and remake lists with documented sources; several hundred pairs. *Method:* age distribution of documented sources against an availability-weighted null; then the same for units, asking how old the earliest carrier of each newly reappearing unit is. *Rival to beat:* word-overlap source attribution; documented sources alone with no text model. *Falsifier:* no excess at 25–35 years over a smooth decay, and unit ages add nothing to the documented-source ages. *Cost:* S to M.

## Fog

- **Search coverage.** The shared WebSearch budget was exhausted after two queries. 2025–26 coverage rests on about fifteen arXiv API queries. A follow-up by Acerbi or others replicating the LLM chain biases across many models may exist; I did not find one.
- **Details tagged from memory** were not confirmed against an abstract: Bentley's z ≈ y√μ and the Evans–Giometto exponents; Henrich's 2004 formula; the direction of the Acerbi–Kendal–Tehrani folktale result; Lambert et al.'s headline finding; Heath et al.'s specifics; Brand et al.'s lyric trends; the Acerbi & Bentley turnover-profile shapes; the −3/2 exponent; Leroi et al.'s argument. Check before any of these enters a design or the paper.
- **Data availability not checked:** the exact OSF repository for Acerbi & Stubbersfield; whether human chain transcripts (Mesoudi 2006, Kashima 2000, Stubbersfield 2015, Bebbington 2017) are public; the split structure of the Upworthy archive; size and licence of a film-plot corpus with box office; open citation data with abstracts. Lyrics corpora have copyright problems.
- **Whether Movva et al. 2025 already ran SAE features on the Upworthy headlines.** I believe so but did not confirm; if true, seed 1 has a published feature-based rival and must beat it too.
- **Is discreteness even measurable on 4-bit 8B models?** The dip-test seed assumes activations are not distorted by quantisation.
- **Circularity in LLM chains.** If the reader model and the generator share training data, "attractors" in unit space may be shared priors, not properties of the text. Human chains are the control; their public availability is the bottleneck.
- **No one has stated what effect size would count as "neurons see more".** The cultural-evolution literature's content-bias effects are modest, so a neuron advantage on top of them will be small; power calculations need a target difference before any of these runs.
- **Activations are not in the repository** (`study/.gitignore` excludes `activations*/`, about 15 GB). Seed 5 is cost S only if they are still on Mike's disk; otherwise it needs a re-extraction, which this sweep may not run.
