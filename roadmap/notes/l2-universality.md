# l2-universality: how true is P2 (cross-model convergence and mapping)?

All literature below was confirmed to exist in this session (arXiv API, publisher page or search hit) unless marked `[UNVERIFIED]`. Numbers quoted from papers were read from the paper's HTML via a fetch-and-summarise tool, so check a number against the PDF before it goes into a paper.

## Bottom line
- P2 as worded ("different models converge on the same neurons") is the weakest of three possible readings, and its own lead citation says so. Gurnee et al. 2024 found 1.23% (GPT2-medium), 1.26% (Pythia-160M) and 4.16% (GPT2-small) of neurons universal, across random seeds of the SAME architecture and data, and concluded this is "another piece of evidence that individual neurons are not the appropriate unit of analysis for most network behaviours".
- Verdict by level. Geometry (which texts sit near which): true, but a low bar, partly produced by the data rather than the models, and the "converging with scale" trend is now contested (Gröger et al. ICML 2026; Koepke et al. 2026). Features/directions: partly true, a shared core of roughly 20 to 50% of dictionary features matchable across families at mid depth, with an instrument (SAEs) that only reproduces about 30% of its own features across seeds. Individual neurons: mostly false; a small head of high-norm, word-like units.
- The study's own numbers agree with the literature and cut against the neuron reading. Qwen3-8B to Llama 3.1 8B twins: top 33 memes median r 0.82, but word-matched non-meme units 0.86, and the same twin pairs fall to 0.14 on name-free text, below word-matched controls at 0.56 (`study/results/robustness/nulls.json`, `f2_agreement/sets/top33`).
- The three papers cited for P2 do not support P2 as stated. Gurnee is evidence against. Huh et al. is about kernels (distances between inputs), not units. Jha et al. (vec2vec) is about the output vectors of sentence-embedding encoders, not LLM internals, and is fragile: 3 of 15 seeds converged for the one cross-backbone pair whose seed stability was tested (e5 to gtr; 14 of 15 for the same-backbone pair e5 to gte), 1M embeddings and 1 to 7 GPU days per pair.
- Mapping between Qwen3-8B and Llama 3.1 8B is practical and cheap IF it is done on directions or subspaces with paired texts (the same sentences through both models), pooled to the sentence, at mid depth. That exact model pair has already been joined by a crosscoder (Jiralerspong and Bricken 2026). Unpaired methods (vec2vec) are unnecessary here because paired data are free.
- The trap for memetics: universality selects for words. The units that match best across models are the ones a single word reproduces. Any P2 test must show cross-model agreement beyond what shared vocabulary predicts.

## Findings

### Level 3: same individual neurons
- Gurnee, Horsley, Guo, Kheirkhah, Sun, Hathaway, Nanda, Bertsimas 2024 (TMLR; arXiv:2401.12181). Method: pairwise Pearson correlation of neuron activations over 100M tokens of the Pile test set, five GPT-2 seeds. Universal = excess correlation above 0.5, where excess = mean max correlation across models minus the same against a random rotation of the layer. Result: 1253 of 98,304 neurons in GPT2-medium (the paper prints 1.23%; 1253/98,304 works out to 1.27%, and both figures are the paper's own); Pythia-160M 1.26%; GPT2-small 4.16%. The authors say there is "no principled threshold". Universal neurons have large weight norm and high activation skew and kurtosis (the profile of sparse, monosemantic-looking units), and in late layers are mostly "prediction neuron" variants. The paper notes that cross-family comparison is hard because of tokenisation differences. Source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2401.12181 (same text at https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12181v1; TMLR 2024 acceptance confirmed on OpenReview).
- Nandan et al. 2025, "Universal Neurons in GPT-2: Emergence, Persistence, and Functional Impact" (arXiv:2508.00903, https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00903; authors Nandan, Chou, Kurakula, Blondin, Zhu, Sharma, O'Brien; NeurIPS 2025 Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop poster): same criterion on five GPT-2 small seeds at five training checkpoints, over 5M tokens; ablating universal neurons hurts loss more per neuron than ablating others (the abstract confirms "significant functional impacts ... measured via cross entropy loss"; the per-neuron comparison with other neurons is `[UNVERIFIED]`). Still same architecture.
- Li, Yosinski, Clune, Lipson, Hopcroft 2015, "Convergent Learning: Do different neural networks learn the same representations?" (ICLR 2016; arXiv:1511.07543, https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.07543): the founding result. Some features are learned reliably across seeds, others are not, and some are only matched by a group of units spanning a subspace rather than one to one.
- Dravid, Gandelsman, Efros, Shocher 2023, "Rosetta Neurons: Mining the Common Units in a Model Zoo" (ICCV 2023, pp. 1934-1943; arXiv:2306.09346, https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09346): mines units shared across eight vision models (supervised ResNet50, DINO ResNet50 and ViT, MAE, CLIP ResNet50, BigGAN, StyleGAN-2, StyleGAN-XL) by correlating and clustering. It shows shared units exist across architectures and objectives; it is a mining exercise, not a census. I did not confirm the count of Rosetta neurons.
- Cui et al. 2026, "Do LLMs and VLMs Share Neurons for Inference? Evidence and Mechanisms of Cross-Modal Transfer" (arXiv:2602.19058, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19058): the headline is that "more than half of the top-activated units during multi-step inference are shared" between an LLM and a vision-language model built on the same language backbone (for example Qwen2.5-Math against Qwen2.5-VL), which is a fine-tune comparison and not cross-family. It also reports a "non-negligible proportion" of shared neurons even between models without the same language backbone, by overlap of top-activated units (a looser measure than correlation matching); no number for that case was found in the main text.
- I found NO published cross-family neuron census on 7B to 8B LLMs. The study's own twin analysis is the nearest thing in existence.
- The study's twin numbers (`study/results/robustness/nulls.json`, `f2_agreement/sets`):
  - top 33 memes: median twin r 0.8236; sparsity-matched random units 0.4016; lexically matched non-meme units 0.8608; paired advantage over lexical controls 0.048, 25 of 33 wins, p = 0.0074. Best single word reproduces the Qwen unit at median r 0.8548.
  - same twin pairs on name-free text: 0.1414, against 0.3297 (sparsity-matched) and 0.5556 (lexical controls).
  - top 200 memes: 0.6364 against 0.4074 (sparsity) and 0.5728 (lexical); name-free 0.1423 against 0.4408 (lexical).
  - 27 concept-labelled memes: 0.6207 against 0.4584 lexical (20 of 27, p = 0.001); name-free 0.3426 against 0.3773 (advantage gone).
  - All 14,843 families: median twin r 0.40, against 0.36 for active non-meme units and 0.14 with sentences shuffled (`study/paper/paper.md`, section 3.3).
  - Revenge units in the two models correlate 0.3871 (`nulls.json`, `f3_wording/revenge_units_corpus_r`).
- Read against Gurnee's bar (excess correlation above 0.5), the median study meme is not universal: 0.82 minus 0.40 is 0.42 for the top 33, and 0.40 minus 0.14 is 0.26 across all families. The baselines differ (sparsity-matched units, not a random rotation), so this is an analogy, not a replication.
- A twin found by taking the best of roughly 917,000 candidate units, and scored on the same 3,700 sentences it was chosen on (`study/paper/paper.md`, sections 2.2 and 2.4), is an in-sample maximum over candidates; such maxima inflate. Gröger et al. 2026 make the same point about maxima over layer pairs and correct it with a permutation null over the whole selection step.
- Verdict: MOSTLY FALSE as a general claim. Roughly 1 to 5% of neurons within one architecture; unknown but plausibly similar or lower across families. The matched minority is biased toward frequent, lexical, high-norm units.

### Level 2: same features or directions
- Lan et al. 2024 (arXiv:2410.06981, now titled "Quantifying Feature Space Universality Across Large Language Models via Sparse Autoencoders"). Matches SAE features by activation correlation, then scores the matched spaces. Gemma-2-2B vs Gemma-2-9B (L11 vs L21): SVCCA 0.70 (random 0.009) but RSA only 0.195, with only about 20% of features kept after filtering (the paper says "approximately 10-30% (and typically 20%)"; the filter drops non-concept features, many-to-one matches and pairs with correlation below 0.1). Llama 3-8B-Instruct vs Llama 3.1-8B (L25): SVCCA 0.3, after keeping only 7% of feature pairs. Concept subspaces score higher (Pythia emotions 0.83 against random 0.15). Limits the authors state: only same-tokenizer (mostly same-family) pairs; SAEs "cannot do this consistently"; middle layers best, early and late layers worse; custom SAEs kept only 2 to 4% of features. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06981v3 (numbers rechecked against v4, https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06981v4). Status: unreviewed. OpenReview lists the earlier-titled version ("Sparse Autoencoders Reveal Universal Feature Spaces Across Large Language Models") as a rejected ICLR 2025 submission and a retitled version as withdrawn from ICLR 2026. Authors: Lan, Torr, Meek, Khakzar, Krueger, Barez.
- Paulo and Belrose 2025, "Sparse Autoencoders Trained on the Same Data Learn Different Features" (arXiv:2501.16615; ICLR 2026 poster, confirmed on OpenReview): a 131K-latent SAE trained on an MLP ("a feedforward network") in Llama 3 8B shares only about 30% of latents across random seeds; smaller models and smaller SAEs share more; TopK SAEs are less stable than ReLU/L1. This caps any SAE-based universality number: the ruler changes length between runs.
- Wang, Ge, Shu, Tang, Zhou, He, Qiu 2025, "Towards Universality: Studying Mechanistic Similarity Across Language Model Architectures" (ICLR 2025 poster, confirmed on OpenReview; arXiv:2410.06672, https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06672): SAE features of a Transformer and a Mamba model are mostly similar; induction circuits analogous. Different architecture, same data.
- Thasarathan, Forsyth, Fel, Kowal, Derpanis 2025, "Universal Sparse Autoencoders" (ICML 2025, PMLR 267; arXiv:2502.03714): one shared dictionary trained to reconstruct any of several vision models from any other. Three vision models (DinoV2, SigLIP, ViT), final layer. Also finds model-specific features (DinoV2). The paper itself (Figure 6b caption, https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03714v2) says "The first 200 concepts co-fire between 60-80% of the time"; the body text says the first roughly 100 concepts co-fire 50 to 80% of the time. That is the top 100 to 200 of the 1,000 most energetic concepts per model, out of a dictionary of 6,144, with co-firing declining after that, so it is not a figure for universal concepts in general.
- Lindsey, Templeton, Marcus, Conerly, Batson, Olah 2024, "Sparse Crosscoders for Cross-Layer Features and Model Diffing" (published 25 October 2024, https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/crosscoders/): one dictionary reading and writing two models; self-described as preliminary. I could not retrieve its shared-fraction numbers.
- Minder et al. 2025, "Overcoming Sparsity Artifacts in Crosscoders to Interpret Chat-Tuning" (NeurIPS 2025; arXiv:2504.02922): the standard (L1) crosscoder loss misattributes latents as model-exclusive; fixes proposed (Latent Scaling to flag the problem, BatchTopK loss to reduce it). Jiralerspong and Bricken add that standard crosscoders carry a prior toward shared features; they credit that finding to Mishra-Sharma et al. 2025, which was not looked up in this session `[UNVERIFIED]`.
- Jiralerspong and Bricken 2026, "Cross-Architecture Model Diffing with Crosscoders" (arXiv:2602.11729): Dedicated Feature Crosscoders with a reserved model-exclusive partition (5% in the headline run), trained between Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, the study's exact pair. Finds Chinese Communist Party alignment features exclusive to Qwen3-8B and American-exceptionalism features exclusive to Llama. Implication: the bulk of a joint dictionary can be shared between these two models, and the differences are specific and nameable. The 5% is a design choice, not a measured exclusive share (the paper also reports 3% and 1% partitions; the 5% partition is 6,533 features). The paper says code to train standard crosscoders and DFCs is in the authors' GitHub repository; released weights were not confirmed.
- Ognev et al. 2026, "SharedSAE: One Feature Dictionary Across Language Models" (arXiv:2609.04344): one dictionary across four 1B-scale base models from different families and tokenizers retains 96.6% of dedicated SAEs' explained variance, with cross-model latent correlations 1.8 times those of post-hoc matching. Authors: Ognev, Vasson, Hu, Inui, Heinzerling. Models (Appendix A of https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04344v1): Qwen3-1.7B-Base, Llama-3.2-1B, OLMo-2-0425-1B and gemma-3-1b-pt, hooked at roughly 85% depth, on English FineWeb-Edu. Note the depth: this is a late-layer result, not a mid-depth one, and the authors say larger scale and other languages are untested.
- Agarwal 2026, "Cross-Architecture Steering Transfer in Language Models: A Systematic Empirical Study" (arXiv:2608.05164; single independent author, unreviewed, treat as provisional): five models (GPT-2-large, Gemma-2-2B, Hermes-3-Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, DeepSeek-LLM-7B-base), TopK SAEs at about 50% depth. Cross-model feature pairs passing validation: 45.5% overall, 48.9% for 7B to 7B pairs, 29.9 to 33.3% for pairs with GPT-2-large; 46.9% for Gemma-2-2B paired with a 7B model. Mean Pearson r is 0.512 (median 0.545) across all 3,308 extracted directed pairs, not among the pairs that pass: the pass threshold is r of at least 0.60, so the mean of the passing pairs must be higher. SAE-free Procrustes after CCA (64 components): cosine 0.895 to 0.956 for 7B to 7B pairs. Transferred steering vectors won 71.0% against native 65.3 to 68.0% (difference not significant, p = 0.14).
- Chen, Merullo, Stolfo, Pavlick 2025, "Transferring Linear Features Across Language Models With Model Stitching" (NeurIPS 2025 spotlight, confirmed on OpenReview; arXiv:2506.06609): an affine map between residual streams transfers SAEs, probes and steering vectors between small and large models WITHIN a family (Pythia-70m to 160m, GPT-2 small to medium, Gemma-2-2B to 9B); transferred SAE as initialisation halves training cost (the paper's words: "can lead to 50% cheaper training runs", shown on the Pythia and GPT-2 pairs). Structural features transfer well; semantic features are polarised (some transfer, some do not).
- Oozeer et al. 2025, "Activation Space Interventions Can Be Transferred Between Large Language Models" (ICML 2025 poster, PMLR 267; arXiv:2503.04429; authors Oozeer, Nathawani, Prakash, Lan, Harrasse, Abdullah): learned mappings carry steering vectors (refusal, backdoor removal) across Llama, Qwen and Gemma. The models are small and instruction-tuned: Llama 3.2 (1B, 3B), Qwen 2.5 (0.5B, 1.5B, 2.5B) and Gemma (2B). The abstract's emphasis is on "using smaller models to efficiently align larger ones", which is mostly within a family; the paper has a separate cross-architecture section (section 5) whose results were not read in this check `[UNVERIFIED: how well the cross-family transfers work]`.
- Chughtai, Chan, Nanda 2023, "A Toy Model of Universality" (arXiv:2302.03025): mixed evidence even in toy settings. Networks draw from a shared family of algorithms, but which ones a given seed learns varies.
- Cross-lingual (one model, many languages). Anthropic, "On the Biology of a Large Language Model" (2025, https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html): middle-layer features are language-agnostic, start and end are language-specific, and shared circuits are "more prominent in Claude 3.5 Haiku than in a smaller, less capable model". No overall percentage given. Also Wendler et al. 2024 (arXiv:2402.10588), Brinkmann et al. 2025 (arXiv:2501.06346), Harrasse et al. 2025 (arXiv:2511.10840). This is the best evidence that features can be word-independent at all, which is what a meme needs.
- Verdict: PARTLY TRUE. A shared core exists across families at mid depth; published match rates run from about 20% (Lan, same family) to about 49% (Agarwal, cross-family 7B). The two numbers are not on one scale: Agarwal's is the share of mutual-nearest-neighbour pairs with Pearson r of at least 0.60, while Lan's is the share of feature pairs left after dropping non-concept features, many-to-one matches and correlations below 0.1. Frequent and concrete features match best. The method ceiling is low (30% seed overlap), and joint-dictionary methods are biased toward finding sharing.

### Level 1: same representational geometry
- Huh, Cheung, Wang, Isola 2024, "The Platonic Representation Hypothesis" (ICML 2024; arXiv:2405.07987): models, including across vision and language, measure distances between inputs in increasingly similar ways as they get more capable. Published as "Position: The Platonic Representation Hypothesis", PMLR 235:20617-20642, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/huh24a.html. Metric: mutual k-nearest-neighbour overlap (k = 10) on 1,024 paired samples from the WIT image-caption set. The best vision-language score is 0.16 on a 0 to 1 scale and the paper itself asks what to make of that. Confirmed in https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07987v5, section "Lots left to explain": "alignment clearly increases but only reaches a score of 0.16 ... The maximum theoretical value for this metric is 1. Is a score of 0.16 indicative of strong alignment with the remaining gap being 'noise' or does it signify poor alignment with major differences left to explain? We leave this as an open question."
- Gröger, Wen, Brbić 2026, "Revisiting the Platonic Representation Hypothesis: An Aristotelian View" (ICML 2026, PMLR 306; arXiv:2602.14486): similarity scores are inflated by model width and depth and by picking the best layer pair. After a permutation calibration, global spectral similarity (CKA) "largely loses its scaling trend"; local neighbourhood overlap (mutual kNN) stays significant. Their revised claim: convergence to shared LOCAL neighbourhood relations, not a shared global geometry.
- Koepke, Zverev, Ginosar, Efros 2026, "Back into Plato's Cave" (arXiv:2604.18572): cross-modal mutual-kNN alignment degrades sharply when the gallery grows from 1,024 to millions; one-to-one image-caption pairing inflates it; the "stronger language models align more with vision" trend does not hold for newer models. Abstract: models "may learn equally rich representations of the world, just not the same one". A news summary gives 13.5% falling to 0.81% at 15M pairs `[UNVERIFIED]`.
- Davari et al. 2023, "Reliability of CKA as a Similarity Measure in Deep Learning" (ICLR 2023; arXiv:2210.16156): CKA is sensitive to outliers and simple transformations and can be pushed high or low without changing model behaviour. Kornblith et al. 2019 (arXiv:1905.00414) is the origin of CKA. Klabunde et al. survey (arXiv:2305.06329) for the menu of measures.
- Dujmović, Bowers, Adolfi, Malhotra 2022, "Obstacles to inferring mechanistic similarity using Representational Similarity Analysis" (bioRxiv, doi:10.1101/2022.04.05.487135): two systems can show the same representational geometry because of structure in the dataset, with no shared mechanism (a "mimic effect").
- Jha, Zhang, Shmatikov, Morris 2025, "Harnessing the Universal Geometry of Embeddings" (arXiv:2505.12540), read from v4 HTML. Translates sentence embeddings between encoders with no paired data (adversarial plus cycle-consistency losses through a shared latent). Models: gtr (T5), e5, gte, stella (BERT), granite (RoBERTa), clip, and one pair with a Qwen3 4B embedder. Best: cosine up to 0.92, top-1 up to 100% on a shuffled gallery of 8,192. Weakest cross-backbone pairs: e5 to gtr cosine 0.74, top-1 0.82. Conditions: 1M embeddings per model (10K gives top-1 0.01; 50K gives 0.81); 1 to 7 GPU days per pair, 20 days for the Qwen pair, about 176 GPU days in total; best of several seeds, with only 3 of 15 cross-backbone runs converging. Out of distribution (MIMIC medical notes) the worst pairs keep cosine 0.66 to 0.82 but top-1 falls to 0.01. Text-to-CLIP: top-1 0.35 one way, 0.72 the other. The tested encoders are 109M to 278M parameters, mostly trained contrastively on overlapping retrieval data with similar objectives.
- Dar 2025, "mini-vec2vec" (arXiv:2510.02348): replaces the adversarial training with a linear pipeline (approximate matching, fit, refine); far cheaper, comparable alignment. One version notes it needs a small set of paired anchors, so check the version before calling it unsupervised. Ancestor: Conneau et al. 2017, "Word Translation Without Parallel Data" (arXiv:1710.04087).
- Moschella et al. 2023, "Relative representations enable zero-shot latent space communication" (ICLR 2023 oral; arXiv:2209.15430): describe each item by its similarities to a fixed anchor set; the description is invariant to rotations and rescalings of the space, which permits zero-shot stitching between independently trained models.
- Bansal, Nakkiran, Barak 2021, "Revisiting Model Stitching" (arXiv:2106.07682) and Lenc and Vedaldi 2014 (arXiv:1411.5908): a functional test. Connect the bottom of model A to the top of model B with one learned linear layer and measure the loss penalty.
- Lee et al. 2025, "Shared Global and Local Geometry of Language Model Embeddings" (arXiv:2503.21073): token embeddings of different LLMs share global and local structure.
- The study's own geometry result (`study/results/robustness/nulls.json`, `f2_trees`): Qwen and Llama distance matrices over 45 works correlate 0.9783. Random non-meme units give 0.8999 to 0.9276. TF-IDF against LSA gives 0.924. Partial correlation controlling for TF-IDF: 0.9243 for memes against 0.8236 to 0.8784 for random units. So near-identical geometry between two models is cheap and mostly reflects the texts; the meme-specific margin over random units is about 0.05 to 0.10.
- Verdict: TRUE in the weak sense (coarse and local neighbourhood structure agree, and linear maps between mid-layer spaces reach cosines near 0.9 with paired data). CONTESTED in the strong sense (one global geometry that all models approach with scale). High scores at this level say little about shared units, as the study's random-unit control shows.

### Conditions under which cross-model mapping works
1. Paired inputs. Every strong result except vec2vec uses the same inputs run through both models. Mike has this for free.
2. A shared pooling unit. Qwen and Llama tokenise differently, so compare at the sentence or passage (max or mean pool). The study already does this ("strongest response in every sentence", `study/paper/paper.md` section 2).
3. Middle layers, around 40 to 60% depth. Early and late layers are tokenizer- and model-specific (Lan et al.; Anthropic multilingual result).
4. Comparable and sufficient scale. Agarwal: pairs at or above 1.7B work, 7B to 7B best; GPT-2-large pairs worst.
5. Same text distribution for fitting and testing. vec2vec top-1 collapses from above 0.8 to 0.01 on medical notes for weak pairs.
6. Frequent, concrete features. Rare or abstract ones are polarised (Chen et al.) and are exactly where the study's twins fail.
7. Subspaces, not units. Linear or affine maps on the residual stream, CCA or a joint dictionary. One-to-one neuron matching is the special case that works for 1 to 5%.
8. Enough samples for the dimension. The residual stream is 4,096 wide in both models (from memory, check the configs); MLP widths are 12,288 per layer in Qwen3-8B and 14,336 in Llama 3.1 8B (derived from the unit counts 884,736 = 36 x 12,288 x 2 and 917,504 = 32 x 14,336 x 2 in `study/paper/paper.md` section 2). The Batman corpus has about 3,700 sentences, too few to fit a full-rank map. Fit on a large outside corpus (100K sentences or more), or reduce to 64 to 512 components first.
9. A calibrated null. Any best-of-many match, layer-pair sweep or similarity score needs a permutation null over the whole selection step (Gröger et al.), and here also a lexical null.

### Mapping methods a small lab can use between Qwen3-8B and Llama 3.1 8B
In rough order of cost. All assume the existing MLX extraction.
- A. Relative representations (no training). Pick 1,000 to 2,000 anchor sentences. Describe each text by its cosine to every anchor, in each model. Compare the two descriptions directly. Hours.
- B. Paired linear map. Mean- or max-pool mid-layer residuals (try Qwen L14 to L22 of 36, Llama L12 to L20 of 32) over 100K or more outside sentences. Fit ridge regression or orthogonal Procrustes (after PCA or CCA to 64 to 512 components). Report held-out retrieval top-1 on a shuffled gallery, at several gallery sizes, because small galleries flatter (Koepke et al.). A day.
- C. Calibrated one-to-one twins (the study's method, tightened). Find twins on half A of a corpus, score on half B. Use mutual nearest neighbours or Hungarian assignment so one Llama unit is not everyone's twin. Report Gurnee's excess correlation against a random-rotation baseline, plus a lexical baseline. A day.
- D. Sparse many-to-one. Predict each Qwen unit from at most k Llama units (lasso) and from the whole layer (ridge). The gap between single-twin R2, k-unit R2 and full-layer R2 says directly whether a meme is a neuron, a few neurons or a direction. A day.
- E. Joint dictionary. A crosscoder or SharedSAE-style dictionary on both models' activations, with a reserved model-exclusive partition (Jiralerspong and Bricken) and the Minder et al. fixes. Check first whether the Qwen3-8B / Llama-3.1-8B crosscoder from arXiv:2602.11729 is released. Days of GPU, or a sentence-pooled version on a Mac in about a week.
- F. Functional check. Map a direction from one model into the other with B, then steer or probe with it (Oozeer et al.; Chen et al.). This is the test Cammarata's paper treats as decisive and the study did not run.
- Not recommended: vec2vec. It solves a problem Mike does not have (no paired data), costs GPU-days per pair, fails most seeds across backbones, and has not been shown on LLM internals.

## What this means for P1 and P2
- P2 needs rewording. Supportable: "Models of similar scale share a large part of their feature subspace at mid depth, and a paired linear map or joint dictionary carries a direction from one to the other." Not supportable: "the same neurons". The paper's sentence "Neurons with similar jobs recur across separately trained models [8]" (`study/paper/paper.md` section 1) is accurate only for the 1 to 5%.
- The Jha citation is being asked to carry more than it can. It shows that outputs of small contrastive sentence encoders can be aligned without pairs, expensively and unreliably. It says nothing about MLP neurons in an 8B chat model.
- The Huh citation supports only the geometry level, and its scaling claim has two 2026 rebuttals. The study's own control (random units reach 0.90 to 0.93) is the same criticism in miniature.
- P1 and P2 pull against each other. Cross-model agreement is highest for units a single word reproduces. If universality is used as a quality filter on memes, it selects word and name detectors, which word counts already measure. The concept memes that would make P1 interesting are where P2 is weakest: their cross-model advantage is 0.62 against 0.46 on original text and gone on name-free text (`nulls.json`, `labelled_concept`).
- Cross-model agreement is necessary and not sufficient for a meme being real. TF-IDF also agrees with itself across any two implementations. The informative quantity is agreement left over after words are controlled, and on that the study has one positive result (concept memes, original text, p = 0.001) and one null (name-free).
- There is a constructive route. The cross-lingual literature shows mid-layer features that fire for a concept regardless of the words. If memes exist as model-internal units, they should be found there, and translation is a cleaner way to remove word overlap than name removal.
- P1 should probably move from "neuron" to "direction or dictionary feature" for the generality claim, while keeping neurons as a cheap readout. Gurnee's own conclusion and the 1 to 5% number point that way. The cost is that SAE-type features are not seed-stable (30%), so the unit is then defined only up to a model and a dictionary run unless a shared dictionary is used.

## Experiment seeds
1. **Twin census with honest nulls.** Question: what share of Qwen3-8B units have a Llama 3.1 8B twin above excess correlation 0.5 on held-out text, and what share survives removing the best word? Data: 200K sentences of non-Batman Wikipedia plot summaries, split in half. Method: sentence max-pool; find twins on half A, score on half B; mutual nearest neighbour; random-rotation baseline as in Gurnee. Rival to beat: for each unit, the best single-word indicator and a 10-word lasso predicting its activation; report twin r after partialling these out. Falsifies neuron-level P2 if under 5% of units pass, or if the passing share drops to the rotation baseline after lexical partialling. Cost: 1 to 2 days on the existing Mac pipeline, no API spend.
2. **Neuron, few neurons or direction?** Question: is a Qwen meme better predicted by one Llama neuron, ten, or a whole layer? Data: same 200K sentences plus the Batman corpus as held-out test. Method: single-twin R2 against lasso (k up to 10) against ridge from the full Llama layer, per meme unit, held out. Rival to beat: ridge from TF-IDF vectors and from an off-the-shelf sentence-embedding model to the same unit. Falsifies "mapping adds something" if words or sentence embeddings predict the Qwen unit as well as Llama's layer does for most meme units. Cost: about 1 day.
3. **Universality through translation.** Question: does cross-model agreement survive when no words are shared? Data: 2,000 plot sentences with LLM translations into Spanish and Chinese (both models read both). Method: fit twins or a linear map on English; test whether the mapped unit fires on the translation of the sentences that drove it; compare within-language and across-language agreement, per meme class. Rival to beat: TF-IDF is at zero by construction, so the rivals are a multilingual sentence-embedding model's cosine and an LLM judge asked whether the sentence expresses the labelled concept. Falsifies the claim if cross-language, cross-model agreement for concept memes is no better than for random sparsity-matched units, or below the multilingual embedding. Cost: 1 to 2 days, translation under 50 US dollars.
4. **Does universality select for words?** Question: how much of the variance in twin r is explained by how word-like a unit is? Data: already committed per-unit fields `r_orig`, `best_word_r`, `name_free_retention` for 200 memes (`study/results/robustness/nulls.json`, `f2_agreement/per_meme`), extended to all 14,843 families. Method: regress twin r on best-word r, firing frequency and layer depth; then test the concept class on the residual. Rival to beat: best-word r alone as the predictor. Falsifies "universality marks real memes" if best-word r explains most of twin r and the concept class adds nothing after it. Cost: hours, no model runs for the 200.
5. **Shared dictionary against neurons against words on heredity.** Question: do features from a joint Qwen-Llama dictionary recover documented sources on name-free text beyond words, where Qwen's neurons failed (p = 0.09 to 0.25 as reported in `study/paper/paper.md` Table 2; underlying rows in `study/results/experiments.json`, `qwen3-8b/E2/residual_test_beyond_words`)? Data: the E2 corpus plus a larger adaptation set from the pre-registration lists. Method: sentence-pooled crosscoder with a model-exclusive partition; rerun the E2 residual test using shared latents only. Rival to beat: TF-IDF cosine, word presence, and a sentence-embedding model, each entered in the same residual test. Falsifies it if shared latents add nothing after words in both models. Cost: 2 to 5 GPU days or about a week on a Mac; less if the arXiv:2602.11729 crosscoder is public.
6. **Mapped-direction functional test.** Question: Llama's revenge unit worked without revenge words and Qwen's did not (r between them 0.39). If Llama's direction is mapped into Qwen by a paired linear map, does Qwen now detect revenge without the word? Data: the study's 12 revenge pairs plus 200 new ones written by an LLM and checked by hand. Method: method B map; score AUC of the mapped direction in Qwen. Rival to beat: cosine to a "revenge" prototype in a sentence-embedding model, and an LLM zero-shot yes/no. Falsifies "features map even when neurons do not" if the mapped direction is at chance, and falsifies "neurons add value" if the embedding or the LLM reader has a higher AUC. Cost: 1 day, under 20 US dollars.

## Fog
- No cross-family neuron census on 8B-class LLMs was found. The fraction of Qwen3-8B neurons with a real Llama 3.1 8B twin is unknown; the study measured it only for selected memes and as a median.
- The study's twin search is in-sample: "its twin in another model is the unit whose responses across the 3,700 sentences correlate most strongly with it" (`study/paper/paper.md`, section 2), and the reported r is that same maximum over roughly 917,000 candidates on the same 3,700 sentences. The matched-unit and shuffled baselines run the same search, so comparisons between groups are fair, but every absolute twin r (0.82, 0.40) is inflated by an unknown amount. No held-out twin r exists.
- Unknown whether 4-bit quantisation changes neuron-level matching.
- Numbers I could not confirm: the Platonic paper's best vision-language alignment score (about 0.16, from memory); Koepke's 13.5% to 0.81%; the Universal SAE 60 to 80% co-firing figure; the shared-latent fraction in Anthropic's crosscoder note; the count of Rosetta neurons; the four models in SharedSAE.
- Agarwal 2026 is a single-author preprint; all models share one corpus and the disjoint-corpus control is not reported. Its 45 to 49% could be optimistic.
- Whether the Qwen3-8B / Llama-3.1-8B crosscoder weights or code from arXiv:2602.11729 are public was not checked. If they are, seed 5 gets much cheaper.
- All feature-level universality numbers depend on a correlation threshold (0.5 to 0.7) and on which features are kept. There is no agreed definition of "the same feature", so percentages across papers are not comparable.
- Nobody has shown that cross-model-matched features beat a bag of words at a downstream cultural or textual task. That comparison is absent from the universality literature; the Batman study is unusual in having run it.
- The direction of convergence over time is unsettled: Huh et al. say alignment rises with capability; Koepke et al. say not for newer models. Whether Qwen3 and Llama 3.1 are more or less alike than their predecessors is unknown.
- Cammarata's "already clean" claim was not examined here for what it says about cross-model stability; that belongs to the P1 notes.
