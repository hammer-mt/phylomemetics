# The biologist's toolkit after sequencing, and the memetic analogue of each tool (key: l7-biology-toolkit)

Scope: 37 tools mapped to neuron-defined memes, then nine history-of-science moments. No experiments were run. Study numbers name their committed file. Literature was confirmed this session through Crossref, the arXiv API or a live page unless marked `[UNVERIFIED]`. An independent citation check re-ran every DOI and arXiv ID on 2026-11-08; its changes are listed under "Check log" at the end.

## Bottom line

- **A neuron readout of a text is an expression profile, not a genome.** It has no loci, no alleles and no silent sites. Tools that work on presence/absence matrices port today (gene-content trees, pangenome tiers, Fst, admixture, ABBA-BABA, heritability, GWAS, Price equation). Tools that need a neutral class of change (dN/dS, McDonald-Kreitman, molecular clocks, Tajima's D, coalescent) are blocked until "synonymous" change is defined and calibrated.
- **Almost every tool in the table can be run on words, and many already have been** (Canterbury Tales stemma 1998, baby-name drift 2003 and 2004, MemeTracker 2009, Facebook meme mutation 2016, drift vs selection in English 2017, waka phylogeny from neural LM vectors 2025). Neuron memes are not a prerequisite for a quantitative memetics. They have to earn their place tool by tool against the word version.
- **The study's own results put the unit at "ties with words"**: heredity p = 0.0026 (Qwen) and 0.00017 (Llama) for neurons, 0.00031 for TF-IDF cosine and 0.0018 for word presence; neurons add nothing beyond words in Qwen (p = 0.089 to 0.25) and something in Llama (p = 0.010 to 0.033). All from `study/results/experiments.json` (the exact p values are the `chance.joint_relabelling_exact.p` fields under each model's `E2.primary` and under `lexical_comparators`; the 10,000-permutation `p_one_sided` fields in the same file read 0.0040, 0.0002, 0.0007 and 0.0021; the beyond-words ranges are under `E2.residual_test_beyond_words`).
- **The one family of tools with no word-level equivalent is interventional**: knock-out and steering of a unit inside the writer model (the CRISPR screen), plus deep mutational scans and directed evolution with a unit readout. The study did not use it (labels were "not validated by steering", `study/paper/paper.md` section 2.3). This is where the next money should go.
- **History-of-science verdict**: memetics already has its Linnaeus (ATU index, Thompson's Motif-Index, TV Tropes), its Darwin (Dawkins; Cavalli-Sforza and Feldman; Boyd and Richerson) and its Kimura (Bentley, Hahn and Shennan 2004). The theory of the modern synthesis was written before the unit existed. What it lacks is a Mendel (clean units in controlled crosses) and a Sanger (a cheap reader with a known error rate). Neuron memes are a candidate Sanger and could support a Mendel. They cannot be a Watson and Crick for human culture: an LLM neuron is an instrument, not the substrate in a human head.
- **Cheapest decisive next steps**: (1) a known-phylogeny benchmark with paraphrase at every copy, (2) the neutral null before any selection claim, (3) ABBA-BABA and pangenome-openness from the committed presence matrix (near zero cost), (4) one steering knock-out in a transmission chain.

## Findings

### 1. What kind of data a neuron-meme profile is

- The study records, for each of 884,736 Qwen3-8B units, the strongest response per sentence, and calls a unit present in a work when the mean of its top three sentences beats all but one of the reference films (`study/paper/paper.md` section 2.3; `study/results/qwen3-8b/memes.json.gz` field `threshold_rule`: "beats 69 of 70 baseline films"). That is a thresholded expression matrix: works by units, binary.
- Biology has names for exactly this data type. A tree from shared gene presence is a gene-content phylogeny (Snel, Bork and Huynen 1999, doi:10.1038/5052). The core/common/optional tiers are a pangenome (Tettelin et al. 2005, doi:10.1073/pnas.0506758102). The study's tiers: 56 core, 194 common, 14,593 optional families out of 14,843 (`study/results/canon.json`, fields `tiers`, `n_core`, `n_optional`).
- The committed `memes.json.gz` keeps per-family presence, z and top sentences, not the full sentence-by-unit matrix. Every positional tool below (alignment, synteny, recombination painting) needs a re-extraction.
- The presence rule has about a 3% false-positive rate per unit per work by construction (`study/paper/paper.md` section 2.3). Any frequency-spectrum statistic (Tajima's D, singleton counts) will be dominated by that noise unless modelled.
- The texts are Wikipedia plot summaries written by modern editors, not the original artefacts. A "molecular clock" on them measures the summariser as much as the work.

### 2. The four tiers

- **Tier A, ports today (presence matrix is enough):** gene-content phylogeny, pangenome, barcoding, Fst and admixture, LD, ABBA-BABA, ancestral states, GO-style annotation, families, metagenomic profiling, neutral frequency tests, Price equation, heritability, GWAS.
- **Tier B, needs a coordinate (sentence position):** alignment, BLAST with local hits, synteny, recombination breakpoints, sweeps as haplotype blocks.
- **Tier C, needs a neutral class and a mutation model:** dN/dS, McDonald-Kreitman, clocks, coalescent, Tajima's D. The obvious neutral class is "wording change that leaves concept units unchanged". The study's rewording test says the class is small: 11 of 31 Qwen memes and 9 of 31 Llama twins survive a rewording, against 5.0 and 5.7 expected under a mismatched-rewording null (`study/paper/paper.md` section 3.4, from `study/results/wording.json`).
- **Tier D, interventional, LLM-native:** deep mutational scanning, directed evolution, CRISPR-style knock-out and steering, experimental phylogenetics with a known tree, transmission chains. Cammarata's page reports steering checks that "line up closely" with reading the top examples (https://already-clean.goodfire.com/; local copy a saved copy of the essay, line 92). Checked: the phrase is there, but in a walk-through of how one would read an example neuron ("check its steering effects ... which line up closely"). The page gives no measured agreement rate between steering and reading top examples.

### 4. Prior art that already runs these tools on culture without neurons

- Stemmatics: The Canterbury Tales (Barbrook et al. 1998); artificial manuscripts with a known true tree (Spencer et al. 2004); chain letters (Bennett, Li and Ma 2003).
- Folktales: Little Red Riding Hood (Tehrani 2013, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0078871); Indo-European tales (da Silva and Tehrani 2016); population structure of a folktale (Ross et al. 2013); folktale diffusion vs genomic distance (Bortolini et al. 2017); Cinderella, explicitly called "phylomemetic" (Sakamoto Martini et al. 2023).
- Neutral models and selection tests: baby names (Hahn and Bentley 2003; Bentley et al. 2004); English verbs (Lieberman et al. 2007; Newberry et al. 2017); the warning paper (Karjus et al. 2020); turnover under bias (Acerbi and Bentley 2014).
- Online memes: MemeTracker (Leskovec et al. 2009); quotation mutation (Simmons et al. 2011); Facebook meme variants with mutation rates (Adamic et al. 2016).
- Neural vectors, not neurons: waka poetry phylogeny from a neural language model (Tanaka 2025); diachronic embeddings (Hamilton et al. 2016); SAE features tracking concept change in press corpora (HistLens, arXiv:2604.11749).
- LLMs as organisms: transmission chains (Acerbi and Stubbersfield 2023; Perez et al. arXiv:2403.08882 and arXiv:2407.04503); model phylogeny (PhyloLM, arXiv:2404.04671); trait transmission between models (Cloud et al., arXiv:2507.14805).
- A web search this session found no work building phylogenies of texts from SAE features or neurons. That gap is real, but the neighbouring ground is crowded.

## What this means for P1 and P2

**P1 (a meme can be measured by which neurons fire).** Judged as a biologist would judge a new marker:

- Discrete and countable: yes. 14,843 families, addressable (`canon.json`).
- Heritable along documented descent: yes, in two models, but word presence is equally heritable (`experiments.json`).
- Independent of wording, which is the property that would make it a gene-level marker and not a spelling-level one: weak. 11 of 31 survive rewording against 5.0 expected (`paper.md` section 3.4).
- Causal handle: untested in the study.
- Known error rate: about 3% false presence per unit per work by design; no sensitivity estimate against planted truth.
- Verdict: the unit currently qualifies as a phenotype array that ties with protein gels (words). It is adequate for Tier A tools and unproven for Tier C. The toolkit gives P1 a precise pass mark: recover a known tree better than words when wording is scrambled.

**P2 (models converge, so the measure generalises).** Biology never needed every gene to be universal. One universal marker family was enough for the tree of life. So the weak form of P2 is sufficient for phylogenetics: a few hundred wording-robust units with twins in every model.

- The source paper itself reports universality as a minority property: 1 to 5% of GPT2 neurons across seeds (Gurnee et al., arXiv:2401.12181 abstract).
- In the study the twins are strong where the unit is lexical and weak where it is conceptual: median twin r = 0.82 for the 33 strongest, 24 of them name or place detectors; concept families 0.62 against 0.46 for word-matched controls on original text, and 0.34 against 0.38 with names removed (`paper.md` section 3.3). The two models' revenge units correlate at 0.387 (`study/results/wording.json`, `revenge.cross_model_r`).
- Tree agreement between models is 0.98, 0.92 after controlling TF-IDF, with random non-meme units at 0.82 to 0.88 (`paper.md`, cross-model controls). The margin over random units is thin.
- Verdict: P2 holds for word-like units, which do not need a neural network to measure. For concept units it is unproven.

## Experiment seeds

**S1. Known-phylogeny benchmark.** Question: when the true tree is known and wording is scrambled at every copy, do neurons recover it better than words? Data: 5 seed stories, each copied through a branching tree of 32 leaves by an LLM, in three conditions (verbatim-ish retelling, forced paraphrase, translate and back). Method: gene-content tree per method; Robinson-Foulds distance to truth. Rival: TF-IDF, word presence, multilingual-e5 embeddings, an LLM asked to pair leaves. Falsified if neurons are not better than embeddings in the paraphrase and translation conditions. Cost: a few hundred LLM calls plus about 2 hours of local extraction. Models on Hillis et al. 1992 and Spencer et al. 2004.

**S2. Neutral null first.** Question: does any unit's frequency change across decades exceed random copying? Data: Wikipedia film plot sections by release decade (the pre-registration scout already cached lists). Method: frequency-increment test per unit; turnover rate against the Bentley neutral expectation. Rival: the same test on word types. Falsified if units rejected as non-neutral are the same items a word test flags, or if none survive correction. Cost: one extraction over about 5,000 summaries.

**S3. Deep mutational scan on headlines.** Question: do units predict which headline variant wins, beyond words? Data: Upworthy Research Archive (Matias et al. 2021). Method: within-test paired comparison; unit differences predict click difference; held-out tests. Rival: word features, embedding difference, an LLM picking the winner. Falsified if held-out accuracy is not above embeddings and the LLM reader. Cost: extraction over about 10^5 short texts; hours. Closest prior art: Movva et al. 2025.

**S4. CRISPR knock-out in a transmission chain.** Question: does ablating a named unit in the retelling model remove that meme from retellings and leave the rest? Data: 20 stories x the 11 rewording-robust units. Method: retell with unit ablated, with a random unit ablated, and unmodified; blind LLM and human judges score meme presence. Rival: a prompt instruction to omit the idea; a keyword logit ban. Falsified if ablation does no more than a random unit, or no more than the keyword ban. Cost: local 8B model with hooks; a day.

**S5. ABBA-BABA for crossovers.** Question: can documented borrowing be detected as excess sharing? Data: committed name-free presence matrix; four-taxon sets such as (ordinary film; Superman; Batman v Superman; another Batman film). Method: D statistic with block jackknife over units. Rival: D on word presence. Falsified if D is not positive for documented crossovers, or if words give the same Z. Cost: near zero, no new extraction.

**S6. A dN/dS for stories.** Question: is concept content conserved more than wording along documented descent, and is the core canon most conserved? Data: source/adaptation pairs plus LLM paraphrases of each source as the neutral baseline. Method: omega = (concept-unit change / wording change) in real descent divided by the same ratio under paraphrase. Rival: embedding distance over lexical distance. Falsified if omega for core units is not below omega for optional units, or the embedding ratio gives the same ordering. Cost: low once a wording-robust unit set of 100+ exists; blocked until then.

**S7. Is the Batman pan-memome open?** Question: do new works keep adding new families faster than pseudo-franchises do? Data: committed presence matrix. Method: accumulation curve, power-law exponent as in Tettelin et al. 2005 `[UNVERIFIED attribution]`. (Checker: the 2005 abstract confirms the pan-genome, the core/dispensable split and a "mathematical extrapolation" showing new genes keep appearing, but I could not read the full text to confirm the curve it fitted. The power-law (Heaps' law) exponent as the open/closed criterion is, from memory, Tettelin, Riley, Cattuto and Medini 2008, doi:10.1016/j.mib.2008.09.006, whose abstract says it proposes "a new model of broad applicability"; Wikipedia's "Pan-genome" page confirms that open or closed is judged by the Heaps' law alpha. Cite both.) Rival: word-type accumulation in the same works. Falsified if the exponent equals the word exponent or the pseudo-franchise exponent. Cost: near zero.

**S8. Unit-wide association with reception.** Question: which units associate with box office or rating after controlling genre, year and length, and do they replicate? Data: 5,000+ film plots with Wikidata outcomes. Method: per-unit regression with principal components, discovery and replication halves. Rival: word-level association, embedding ridge, LLM-rated story features. Falsified if nothing replicates, or variance explained is at or below words. Cost: one extraction; hours.

**S9. Mendel's peas.** Question: do planted memes transmit as discrete particles in controlled crosses, and does the unit readout count them correctly? Data: parent stories each carrying two planted memes; 200 LLM-written (and some human-written) blends per cross. Method: count transmitted memes by planted-truth check, unit readout and LLM reader; test independence between memes. Rival: keyword detector; LLM reader. Falsified if unit readout agrees with planted truth less well than the LLM reader, or if activations are graded with no separation. Cost: extends the paused planted-story plan; about a day.

**S10. A 16S set for P2.** Question: is there a unit set with twins in five models that survives name removal and paraphrase? Data: study corpus plus paraphrases; Qwen, Llama and three more open models. Method: reciprocal best twins; tree agreement from the universal set against random sets after controlling TF-IDF. Rival: random units; word presence. Falsified if the universal set is under about 100 units or its tree agreement is no better than random units. Cost: three more extractions.
