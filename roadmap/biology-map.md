## Landmarks

| Landmark | When | What it gave biology | The memetic version | Where memetics stands | Design |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Linnaeus | 1735 | A naming and ranking system, with no mechanism. | Already exists: folktale type and motif indexes, TV Tropes. Neurons give an automatic version, with labels only partly checked. | exists by hand; automatic version unvalidated | AT-25 |
| Darwin | 1859 | Descent with modification, plus selection. | Already exists by analogy: Dawkins, Cavalli-Sforza and Feldman, Boyd and Richerson. The idea is not what is missing. | done long ago |  |
| Mendel | 1866 | Inheritance in discrete particles, shown by ratios in controlled crosses of seven clean traits. | The right next target. A few clean units, planted in stories and crossed under control. Mendel picked 7 traits, not 14,843. | not attempted; planted stories are the true-breeding lines | AT-23 |
| Mendeleev | 1869 | An ordering whose gaps predicted elements nobody had found. | A table of memes whose gaps predict workable combinations nobody has used. Needs a low-dimensional structure in unit space. Nobody has shown one. | speculative | AT-28 |
| The modern synthesis | 1918 to 1950 | Mendel and Darwin joined by population genetics. | The mathematics was written without a unit. Units would supply its parameters: copying fidelity and selection strength. Comes after the Mendel and Kimura steps. | later | CE-24 |
| Beadle and Tatum | 1941 | One gene, one enzyme: a clean rule that later needed a finer unit (split genes, alternative splicing). | The essay's estimate that roughly 90% of neuron poles have one main job, and the companion paper's finer count a month later in a different model: 47% of neuron poles have one role and 69% one dominant role. For the rest it shows in two worked cases how context tells the roles apart, and says it has not established how common that is. A pole is the wrong trait to build a family tree from wherever it has two roles. | rule stated, already being revised | LI-S13 |
| Avery, Hershey and Chase | 1944 to 1952 | Which substance carries heredity: strip components and see what still transmits. | Name removal was step one: heredity survived without names. Step two, strip the wording by paraphrase or translation, is not done and is cheap. | step one done | LI-22 |
| Tinbergen | 1951 | The supernormal stimulus: a dummy that triggers an instinct harder than the real thing. | Goodfire's agents wrote text that fired 120 of 128 neuron poles harder than anything in a 10-billion-token corpus. If the unit keys on an idea, readers will rate that text a stronger example. If it keys on a token, they will not. | shown for neurons, untested for ideas | IN-31 |
| Watson and Crick | 1953 | A physical structure that explains copying. | Not available for human culture: a model neuron is an instrument, not the substrate in a human head. Possible for ideas passed between models, where weights can be read. | out of reach for human minds | AT-22 |
| Lewontin and Hubby | 1966 | The first cheap measure of variation, applied widely. | Word overlap and TF-IDF. Crude, cheap, and they work. This is the bar every unit has to clear. | delivered by words | IN-30 |
| Kimura | 1968 | The neutral null: most change is drift, so selection must beat that baseline. | Already exists for culture (random copying of baby names and pottery). It must be fitted before any selection claim. The one selection test here had 27% power. | theory exists; not yet applied here | PO-23 |
| Woese and Fox | 1977 | One universal marker gave one tree of life. | This is premise P2: a set of units present in every model and robust to wording. Only 1 to 5% of neurons recur across retrained models. | the hope behind P2; weak so far | UN-21 |
| Sanger | 1977 | Cheap, accurate reading with a known error rate. | The most natural role for neuron reading. Not yet earned: the reader mostly reads words and has no stated error rate against ground truth. | this is where memetics is now | IN-21 |
| Human Genome Project | 1990 to 2003 | A reference catalogue. | For models, catalogues of neuron descriptions exist. A reference list of validated meme units for one public model is cheap to build. A parts list is not function. | buildable now | AT-21 |
| The minimal genome | 2016 | A cell stripped to the genes it cannot live without, some with unknown function. | A model run on the strongest 8% of its neuron activations per token keeps most of its ability: next-word loss rises 2.9%, though six of ten reasoning, knowledge and coding benchmarks fall, by up to 20.9 points, until it is fine-tuned.
