# CE-26: Do meme sets grow and ratchet in chains?

**Area:** Cultural evolution, re-run · **Status:** design registered 8 November 2026, not run · **Priority:** 3.0 of 5

## Question

In chains where each writer learns a hidden rule set from the previous guide plus a few new examples, does the set of units in the guide grow and keep what it gained, in step with measured skill?

## Prediction

Given pilot chains where skill rises 15 points over 10 generations, length-adjusted unit repertoire tracks the number of rules a guide supports (rank correlation at least 0.5, and 0.1 above word-type count) on chains using a renamed game. Units are lost at most half as often from intact guides as from guides missing 40% of sentences.

## What would refute it

Repertoire correlation below 0.3, or within 0.05 of word-type count or length, or equal loss rates in the two arms. 60 chains by 10 generations by 2 arms: 1,200 guides; a chain-level bootstrap on 40 held-out chains should detect a 0.1 correlation gap at about 0.8 power, to confirm by simulation.

## Data

Built, nothing downloaded: an invented card game with 12 coded rules in two skins (same rules, different names for suits and actions), examples generated by program, guides written by two open models to a fixed word budget. Skill is a frozen solver's accuracy on 144 held-out judgments, twelve per rule, given only the guide. Texts released CC BY 4.0.

## Method

Units chosen on 20 development chains in the first skin, frozen; scored on 40 in the second. Per guide: repertoire size after regressing out length. Per unit: gains and losses. Truth: a rule is supported when the solver gets 10 of 12 judgments right. Nulls: guides shuffled across chains within generation; random firing-rate-matched units. Reader differs from writers and solver.

## Cheap rival it must beat

Word-type count, guide length, embedding-cluster count, an LLM asked to list the rules a guide states, random units, mean-pooled residual norm. Most dangerous: the rule-listing LLM, which reads rules directly; then length, because a maximum over tokens rises with length. If length wins, repertoire counts in the study need the same correction.

## Premise it tests

P1. Shows whether a unit count measures accumulated ideas or only text size. A pass licenses growth and ratchet claims for franchises; a fail says the canon tiers partly count length.

## Biology analogue

Gene gain and loss along a lineage; open pangenome

## Cost

M. Game code, 1,200 generated guides and about 173,000 short solver calls; one to two weeks.

## Payoff

A validated way to count accumulation, with loss rates under high and low fidelity, the quantity the ratchet argument turns on.

## Main risk

Invented rules may have no dedicated units, so the eligible set is generic and the rule-listing LLM wins.

## Reviewer note

Replaced the one-sentence arm, where loss is forced by length, with sentence deletion at a fixed word budget; raised solver judgments from 4 to 12 per rule; test chains use a renamed game so word lists cannot transfer. Weakness: invented rules may have no dedicated units.
